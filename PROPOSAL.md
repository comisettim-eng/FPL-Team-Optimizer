Fantasy Premier League (FPL), is played by ten million users worldwide, each managing a 15-player squad under strict constraints, including a £100 million budget, positional limits (2 goalkeepers, 5 defenders, 5 midfielders, 3 forwards), and a maximum of three players per real-life team. The players chosen in the game then accumulate points, based on the real-life performance of these players in the real Premier League. Selecting the optimal team composition that maximizes expected points while respecting these limits is a complex optimization problem. 

This project aims to design a data-driven system that identifies the most efficient FPL squad and transfer strategy through systematic data cleaning, metric computation, optimization, and evaluation. The process will begin by collecting and cleaning player data from the official FPL API using python libraries such as pandas or NumPy. This step ensures data reliability and handles missing or inconsistant values while standardizing variables such as player names, positions, prices, and performance indicators. Once the data pipeline is established, I will compute key performance metrics such as points-per-million, average points per game, form, and fixture difficulty. These metrics will serve as the foundation for comparing player value and performance consistency. To visualize these relationships, I will use matplotlib to produce clear and informative graphs highlighting patters such as cost-efficiency versus form, positional performance distributions, and budget allocation trade-offs. 

Using these computed metrics, I will then build a model that generates an opitimized FPL team for a single gameweek at a time. The model will apply the official FPL constraints ( Budget, positional quotas, and team limits) and aim to maximize total projected points using linear or mixed integer programming through the PulP library. Once functional, I will evaluate how well this optimizer performs compared to the average player over the previous weeks, as well as compared to simpler value-based heuristics (such as selecting players purely in terms of points-per-million) effectively back-testing the optimizer's accuracy and efficiency. 

After validating the single week version, I will expand the project to include machine-learning-based point predictions using scikit-learn. These models will estimnate future points based on features such as recent form, playing time and fixture difficulty. Finally, I will integrate multi-week transfer planning, allowing the opitimizer to consider future fixtures and transfer decisions over several gameweeks while factoring in transfer costs and cumulative points. The performance of this extended system will once again by evaluated against the basic heuristics, the historical average player score, as well as the other, simpler model to determine how much predicitive modeling and strategic planning improves overall results. Overall, this project offers a realistic and technically challenging opportunity to demonstrate data processing, optimization, visualization, software engineering, and evaluation techniques while building a tool with genuine practical relevance for millions of FPL players.
